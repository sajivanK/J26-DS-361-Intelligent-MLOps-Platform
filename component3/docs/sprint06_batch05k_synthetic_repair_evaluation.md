## 1. Executive Summary & Scientific Verdict

The primary research objective of Sprint 06 Batch 5K was to determine whether augmenting an outdated source model with synthetic post-drift data ($K=50$) generated by **TVAE**, **CTGAN**, or **TabDDPM** provides a credible downstream utility advantage over authentic-only adaptation controls:
1. **Unadapted source model ($B0$):** $F1_{\text{dev}} = 0.4055$
2. **Naive authentic pooling ($B1\text{-}B200$):** $F1_{\text{dev}} = 0.4150$
3. **Authentic Random OverSampling ($B2\text{-}B200\text{-}K200$):** $F1_{\text{dev}} = \mathbf{0.4497}$
4. **Target-only adaptation ($B4\text{-}B200$):** $F1_{\text{dev}} = 0.7660$

### Scientific Verdict: NEGATIVE FINDING (Authentic OverSampling Dominates Synthetic Augmentation)
* **Synthetic vs. Unadapted Baseline ($B0$):** Adding 50 synthetic records from TVAE ($F1=0.4324$, $\Delta = +0.0269$), CTGAN ($F1=0.4192$, $\Delta = +0.0137$), or TabDDPM ($F1=0.4218$, $\Delta = +0.0163$) yields modest numerical gains over the unadapted source model ($B0=0.4055$). However, after Holm-Bonferroni multiplicity correction, none of these differences reach statistical significance ($p_{\text{adj}} \ge 0.6930$).
* **Synthetic vs. Authentic Pooling ($B1\text{-}B200$):** Synthetic augmentation marginally outperforms naive unweighted pooling ($B1=0.4150$) by $+0.0042$ to $+0.0174$ F1, but all differences are statistically indistinguishable from chance ($p_{\text{adj}} = 1.0000$).
* **Synthetic vs. Authentic Random OverSampling ($B2\text{-}B200\text{-}K200$):** **Every synthetic arm is strictly inferior to authentic Random OverSampling ($B2=0.4497$)**:
  - TVAE: $F1 = 0.4324$ ($\Delta = \mathbf{-0.0173}$, 95% CI: $[-0.0429, +0.0045]$)
  - TabDDPM: $F1 = 0.4218$ ($\Delta = \mathbf{-0.0279}$, 95% CI: $[-0.0578, -0.0029]$)
  - CTGAN: $F1 = 0.4192$ ($\Delta = \mathbf{-0.0305}$, 95% CI: $[-0.0627, -0.0039]$)
* **Core Conclusion:** Synthetic tabular data generation from small post-drift sample sizes ($N=200$) **fails to provide any measurable, statistically credible advantage** over simply replicating authentic post-drift records. In fact, training-distribution distortion introduced by deep generative approximations acts as negative inductive bias relative to pure empirical oversampling.

## 3. Candidate Availability & Feasibility Assessment

### 3.1 Seven-Tier Candidate Quality Funnel
Every generated candidate underwent sequential multi-tier screening:
1. **Tier 1 (Structural Correctness):** Schema matching, 0 NaNs, 0 Infs, valid categorical domains, valid `Churn` $\in \{\text{"No"}, \text{"Yes"}\}$.
2. **Tier 2 (Hard Physical Constraints):** `tenure` $\in [0, 72]$, `MonthlyCharges` $\ge 0$, `TotalCharges` $\ge 0$.
3. **Deduplication:** Removal of intra-sample duplicates.
4. **Exact Match Screening:** Zero identical 20-column matches against $B200$.
5. **Forbidden Feature Check:** Zero 19-column feature signature matches against evaluation splits.
6. **Tier 3 (Empirical Plausibility):** Billing ratio $\frac{\text{TotalCharges}}{\max(1, \text{tenure}) \times \text{MonthlyCharges}} \in [0.5, 1.5]$.
7. **Privacy Risk Screening:** Distance to Closest Record (DCR) on min-max normalized continuous features.

### 3.3 Feasibility Resolution of $K=50$ and $K=200$
- **$K=50$ Feasibility:** **FEASIBLE for all three generators.** TVAE satisfied $K=50$ directly from the initial pool (65 available). CTGAN and TabDDPM required sampling $N=500$ with fixed seed 42, yielding 52 and 83 plausible unique candidates respectively. The first 50 accepted candidates were saved to frozen CSV artifacts.
- **$K=200$ Feasibility:** **PRE-REGISTERED AS INFEASIBLE.** Under the strict 7-tier eligibility policy, CTGAN achieved only a 10.4% plausible yield and TabDDPM achieved 16.6%. Obtaining 200 plausible unique candidates would require generating $\sim 2,000$ CTGAN records and $\sim 1,200$ TabDDPM diffusion samples. On CPU hardware, this would violate resource budgets and severely distort continuous marginal distributions. Per Step 3 and 5 of the specification, missing candidates were **never** silently replaced with authentic records. Consequently, extended $K=200$ arms ($S4, S5, S6$) were not trained.

## 12. Scientific Limitations

1. **Simulated Shift Dynamics:** The `TELCO_CONCEPT` distribution shift represents a deterministic, piecewise concept inversion designed as an algorithmic stress benchmark. It does not capture smooth, multidimensional consumer behavior drift in live production.
2. **Sample Starvation:** Fitting deep generative models (CTGAN, TabDDPM, TVAE) on only 200 tabular records inevitably limits generative diversity and density estimation accuracy, resulting in low plausible yield (10.4%–32.5%).
3. **Exploratory Development Nature:** All metrics reported are based on $D_{\text{dev\_val}}$ ($N=292$). While isolated from training, repeated development evaluations carry inherent risk of optimism. The final locked test set $D_{\text{target,eval}}$ ($N=493$) remains strictly unaccessed.

## 13. Decision for Next Batch: GO (With Methodological Shift)

**DECISION: GO for Sprint 06 Batch 06 / Next Phase, WITH METHODOLOGICAL RE-ALIGNMENT.**

### Strategic Implications for Component 3
The negative finding obtained in Batch 5K provides crucial scientific clarity:
* **Discard Simple Minority/Post-Drift Augmentation:** Unconditional or naive post-drift synthetic augmentation ($D_{\text{source}} \cup B_k \cup \text{Synth}$) does not beat authentic oversampling ($B2$). Proceeding with claims that deep generative models "solve" concept drift under standard retraining is empirically ungrounded and scientifically invalid.
* **Path Forward:** Future research in Component 3 should focus on:
  1. **Selective/Conditional Repair:** Synthesizing strictly in localized sub-manifolds identified by drift detection modules (Component 1 & Component 2).
  2. **Domain-Weighted Hybrid Repair:** Combining synthetic generation with sample weighting ($B3/B5$).
  3. **Generative Data Valuation & Filtering:** Pruning synthetic candidates using downstream influence functions or validation gradient signals rather than unguided pooling.

### 14.3 Augmentation-Budget Asymmetry ($K=50$ vs $K=200$) & Re-Evaluation Against Matched $B2\_B200\_K50$
* **The Asymmetry:** The primary claim in the original Batch 5K report—that synthetic augmentation was strictly inferior to authentic Random OverSampling—rested on comparing synthetic arms augmented with **$K=50$ records** against an authentic ROS reference ($B2\_B200\_K200$) augmented with **$K=200$ duplicated records**. The authentic control received **four times the augmentation budget** of the generative models.
* **Provenance of Matched Control ($B2\_B200\_K50$):** In Sprint 06 Batch 5I, an authentic ROS model with matched budget $K=50$ was trained and frozen at `component3/models/experimental/telco_rf_b2_b200_k50.joblib`, with historical performance documented in `telco_authentic_controls_dev_metrics_corrected.json`.
* **Matched-Budget Re-Evaluation:**
  When evaluated on identical development validation records ($D_{\text{dev\_val}}$, $N=292$), the matched control achieves:
  $$\text{F1}(B2\_B200\_K50) = \mathbf{0.4028}$$
  Comparing the $K=50$ synthetic arms against this matched-budget control reveals:
  - **$S1\_TVAE\_K50$ ($F1=0.4324$):** Outperforms matched ROS by **$\mathbf{+0.0296}$ F1** (95% CI: $[+0.0058, +0.0582]$, nominal permutation $p = 0.0320$).
  - **$S3\_TabDDPM\_K50$ ($F1=0.4218$):** Outperforms matched ROS by **$+0.0190$ F1** (95% CI: $[-0.0062, +0.0465]$, nominal permutation $p = 0.2174$).
  - **$S2\_CTGAN\_K50$ ($F1=0.4192$):** Outperforms matched ROS by **$+0.0164$ F1** (95% CI: $[-0.0065, +0.0422]$, nominal permutation $p = 0.2974$).
* **Methodological Rectification:** It is scientifically inaccurate to state that synthetic data is inferior to authentic oversampling in general. At an equal sample budget of $K=50$, TVAE synthetic augmentation provides a measurable positive boost over authentic oversampling. The dominance of $B2\_B200\_K200$ reflects the power of quadrupling the adaptation sample count ($K=200$), not an intrinsic flaw in generative modeling at equal sample size.

### 14.4 Statistical Reconciliation: Nominal CIs vs. Multiplicity-Adjusted Permutation Tests
The initial report noted that the 95% bootstrap confidence intervals for CTGAN vs $B2\_K200$ ($[-0.0627, -0.0039]$) and TabDDPM vs $B2\_K200$ ($[-0.0578, -0.0029]$) excluded zero, while their permutation test p-values failed to reach family-wise significance ($p_{\text{adj}} = 0.5715$ and $0.6920$).
* **Statistical Cause:** Percentile bootstrap confidence intervals evaluate a single contrast in isolation without family-wise error rate control. The permutation tests use exact pairwise prediction swaps with finite-sample correction `(extreme + 1) / (R + 1)`, followed by step-down Holm-Bonferroni correction across the 9 pairwise tests.
* **Corrected Interpretation:** When multiple comparisons are formally adjusted, neither superiority nor inferiority is established at $\alpha = 0.05$ against the $K=200$ control. Definite claims of statistical inferiority are ungrounded when multiplicity-adjusted p-values do not reach significance.
